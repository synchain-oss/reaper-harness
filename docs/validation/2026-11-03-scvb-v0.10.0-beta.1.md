## Passthrough part

The layouts `monitor`, `input`, `output`, `system` and `system-params` use every plugin in its default state: no
Input is assigned a channel. In that state the plugins leave the audio untouched, and every render of these layouts
was bit-identical to the same layout without FX, in the master: offline, 1x and online (`system`), at 44.1 and
48 kHz with blocks of 64 and 1024 (`monitor`, `input`), in mono, with every FX bypassed, rendered twice, and after
save and reopen.

This shows passthrough and nothing more. It says nothing about the routing the plugins do once they are set up;
that is the routing part below.

- `system-device`: block size and sample rate read back at 44.1 and 48 kHz with blocks of 64 and 1024, every FX
  reports a latency of 0 (the configuration's default), and the two Output parameters read back with the text
  REAPER shows for them (`Width` 0.5 as `75 %`, `MS Balance` 0.75 as `S50`).
- `instances`: 25 cycles (5 warm-up) of the three plugins added and removed. Handles grew by 40 in every round and
  private memory by 1.9 to 2.8 MB, well within the default limits (150 each), and no track was left.
- `neg-system-quieter-track` (one track 6 dB quieter must not be identical to the `system` layout) came out FAIL as
  expected in every round.

## Routing part

The layout `routing` uses the states from the template: three Inputs on channels 1, 2 and 3 and an Output in group
8 on their folder. Once connected, each Input silences its own track and the Output plays the three tracks on the
bus instead. The criteria and where they come from:

| Row | Criterion | Measured over the 3 rounds |
|---|---|---|
| `routing-offline`, `routing-1x` (48 kHz, blocks of 512) | master RMS within ±0.5 dB of dry and residual at most 12 dBFS; each vocal stem's RMS at least 6 dB below its dry stem | master −0.03 dB; vocal stems −12.19 … −12.11 dB |
| `routing-blocks-*` (44.1 and 48 kHz, blocks of 64 and 1024, offline) | the same | master −0.06 … +0.01 dB; vocal stems −16.58 … −11.10 dB |
| `routing-repeat` (rendered twice) | master as above, against the first render; vocal stems at most −400 dBFS apart | master 0.00 … +0.03 dB against the first render (bit-identical in rounds 1 and 2; in round 3 the two renders differed within the handover, by up to −6.48 dBFS); vocal stems identical in every round |
| `routing-bypass` | every FX bypassed: bit-identical to dry, master and vocal stems | bit-identical in every round |
| `routing-device` | built in: block size and sample rate read back, latency 0, the five states saved back unchanged | PASS in every round |
| `routing-reopen` | every FX bypassed, 3 save and reopen cycles: the five states saved back unchanged, reopened render bit-identical | PASS in every round |
| `neg-passthrough-is-not-routing` | the vocal stem criterion applied to the `system` layout must FAIL | FAIL as expected in every round |

- The master is judged by its level, not sample by sample, because of the handover described in the next section.
  `maxDiffDb` 12 only bounds the handover: at worst a whole copy of the mix is missing or added there, which is the
  mix's own peak (+9.54 dBFS with three tracks).
- Each vocal track's stem must be at least 6 dB below its dry stem, which shows that the Input really silences it.
  The negative case `neg-passthrough-is-not-routing` applies this criterion to the all-default `system` layout and
  came out FAIL as expected in every round (there the vocal stems are identical to dry), so the criterion tells
  routing from passthrough.
- In an earlier run (same tool, layout and states), `routing-repeat` required the master to be
  bit-identical and `routing-reopen` was not bypassed. In one of that run's two rounds, two renders of the same
  project did not have the same master in either row (peak differences −6.48 dBFS and +3.96 dBFS), while the passthrough and bypass rows
  of the same round were bit-identical. The renders of that run and of the three rounds here were kept and compared:
  the differences lie only within the handover (next section), and after it the renders are bit-identical. So
  `routing-repeat` now judges the master by the same levels as the comparison with dry and requires the vocal stems
  to match (`maxDiffDb` −400, the lowest value the tool takes), and `routing-reopen` saves and reopens with every FX
  bypassed: the five states must still be saved back unchanged in all three cycles, and the reopened render must be
  bit-identical.

## The handover

Measured on the kept renders of the three rounds and of the earlier run, and in separate runs with wide limits;
48 kHz unless noted:

- At the start of every render the Inputs pass their tracks through. Then the Output starts to take the mix over
  with a crossfade, and within one block the Inputs go silent. With blocks of 512 the master left the mix without
  plugins at 203 ms and the Inputs went silent at 245 ms, in all 15 renders compared below. With blocks of 1024 the
  Inputs went silent at about 300 ms in every round; with blocks of 64 at a moment that differed from round to round,
  between 90 and 190 ms (at 44.1 and at 48 kHz alike).
- From 100 ms after the Inputs go silent to the end, the master equals the mix without plugins up to rounding
  (−150.5 dBFS), and each vocal track's stem is exactly zero.
- Within the handover the master differs from the mix without plugins: with blocks of 512 the residual is +3.96 or
  +6.24 dBFS (the test signal has a full-scale click on each track at 0.25 s); with blocks of 1024 the master peaks
  above the mix (up to 10.07 dBFS against 9.54), because the Output starts its mix before the Inputs go silent.
- Two offline renders of the same project do not always have the same master within the handover. Of 15 renders
  with blocks of 512 (the comparison with dry and both renders of the repeat row, in each of five rounds), the master
  came out in three forms; two renders that differ do so only between 213 and 331 ms, by up to −6.48 dBFS, and are
  bit-identical from 345 ms on. Their vocal stems were identical in every pair.
- In the earlier run one render of a reopened project handed over later: it followed the mix without plugins
  exactly up to 363 ms, differed from it between 363 and 443 ms, and equalled it up to rounding after that.
- In each round the offline and the 1x render of the layout were bit-identical.
- With blocks of 64 the vocal stems' RMS change against dry ranged over −16.58 … −14.49 dB in the three rounds,
  while with blocks of 1024 it stayed within −11.26 … −11.10 dB.
- In an online render the master stays silent for 1.2 to 1.3 s after the handover (exactly zero for 1179 to 1339 ms
  in four online renders with blocks of 512); an online render of this layout is therefore not judged.

## Side effects

- REAPER's default resource directory was unchanged after every round, by the harness's own check and by a separate
  listing (names, sizes, write times) taken before and after every call.
- The plugins' own user data directories were listed the same way before and after every call: no file or folder
  was added, removed or changed.
- No REAPER process was left after any round.

## Known limitations

- REAPER's evaluation reminder now and then holds up a single launch, which makes that row INCOMPLETE ("stuck on
  dialog license-nag"). Running that row again alone with `-Only` resolves it; the table above says which rows
  needed it.
- The preflight probes for live instances of these plugins once, before the run. There is no hook after the run, so
  an instance in group 8 that someone opens in the middle of a run is not caught.
- The routing layout's online render is not judged (see the handover).
- Routing is judged by levels, not sample by sample, and two renders of the same project were not always
  bit-identical in their master within the handover (see the handover).
- Only the Dummy Audio device was used: no sound card, no real-time playback.
- One machine, one REAPER version, one Windows version.
